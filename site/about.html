About this map

What it is

Type an Edmonton address, or click the map, to see who represents that place on City Council, the public and Catholic school boards, the Legislative Assembly of Alberta and the House of Commons, with a link to each office's own page. It is a reference tool: it does not rank, score or comment on anyone.

How council works

City Council decides as a body. The mayor is also a councillor, and each councillor has one vote each time council votes (Municipal Government Act, sections 154 and 182). Much of what residents meet day to day is delivered by the City's administration, by the Province, or by EPCOR, which runs Edmonton's water delivery, wastewater collection, stormwater facilities and electricity distribution, rather than by an individual councillor. If you are not sure who handles something, 311 is the City's starting point.

How current it is

Every source is re-checked automatically each week. When nothing has changed, the check dates move forward. When something has changed (a new name, a vacant seat, a moved boundary), nothing is published until a person has checked it against the source. A card warns you if its office holder has not been re-checked in 45 days, or its boundaries in 180. Last check: ….

Some dates force a full recheck whatever the last check says:

    Known limits

    • Near a boundary. Each address is placed in its districts using the full-detail boundaries. The map draws simplified lines, accurate to within 5 m. An address within 20 m of a line gets a note pointing to that level's official lookup.
    • Addresses on more than one parcel. A few addresses (8) have parcels in different districts. Each district is shown.
    • Neighbourhoods. A neighbourhood that a boundary line crosses, even for one address, shows every district it touches at that level. Search an address to see which one applies.
    • Gaps and overlaps. Some neighbouring districts in the published boundary files overlap or leave thin gaps, a few square metres in all. A map click that lands in one says so.
    • Provincial boundaries may change. A legislature committee is due to report on new electoral divisions (see the recheck dates above). Until a new map is enacted and an election held under it, this site shows the divisions MLAs represent today.
    • Edge ridings. Some provincial and federal ridings outside Edmonton are included, because a small part of the city falls inside them.
    • Party. Party or caucus is shown for MLAs and MPs only, and only if you turn it on, taken from the Assembly's and the House of Commons' own lists. The City's and the school boards' lists of who holds office carry no party, so those cards have none.

    Your address

    The search runs in your browser. To answer, the page downloads one file for the street you choose from this site; the house number is never sent anywhere, and nothing is logged or stored. A link you copy from the results holds the districts, not the address, unless you tick the box to include it.

    Corrections

    If something looks wrong, the official page linked on the card is authoritative. You can also report it on GitHub.
